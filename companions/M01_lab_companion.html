Module 1 Learning Goals

By the end of this lab, you should be able to open the course notebook with the right kernel and load a CSV file into a pandas table. You should also be able to read records by their ID and save a small output file without changing the raw data. Finally, you should be able to say what business decision a piece of customer text could support.

The main idea is simple: a star rating tells you how a customer felt; the text tells you whether someone still needs help. Every output you make keeps the record ID, so anyone can check it against the original text.

2.5 How long are the complaints?

str.len() counts the characters in each narrative, and describe() summarizes those counts. count is the number of complaints, mean the average length and std the standard deviation (how far lengths typically are from the mean). min and max are the shortest and longest. The 50% row is the median: half are shorter and half are longer. 25% and 75% mark the middle half. .round() rounds to whole numbers. The second cell prints the longest message from Part 1 for comparison.

These tasks are optional and not graded. Try them if you have time.

A. Do the words match the product label?

The product label is the consumer's choice, so it is usually right but not always. This cell shows, for each product, the share of training complaints whose narrative contains the letters card, ignoring capitalization. This also matches parts of longer words, such as cards or discard. The mean of True/False values is a share, because True counts as 1 and False as 0.

# True for each training complaint whose narrative contains the letters "card" (case=False ignores capitals).
mentions_card = train["narrative"].str.contains("card", case=False)

# Group the True/False values by product; the mean in each group is a share.
mentions_card.groupby(train["product"]).mean().round(3)
